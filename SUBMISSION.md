## 5. About the project

### Problem

Dripping taps stay broken for months because a single drop looks like nothing. At the USGS drop size of 0.25 mL, a tap that drips once a second loses about 7,900 litres a year, and a repair request that only says "tap dripping" waits behind everything with a number attached.

### Target users

Students in shared residences and the floor representatives who collect repair requests, tenants who need a number before a landlord will act, and residence staff deciding which tap to fix first.

### Approach

Guttora is one browser tab with no install and no account. The phone's microphone is high-passed at 1 kHz, and our own spectral flux detector counts each drop and checks that the gaps are regular. A 5 mL teaspoon held under the tap gives its real drop size. The app turns the rate and drop size into litres a year, and for a hot tap into kWh and kg CO2e with a national grid factor or the natural gas factor. Every measured tap goes into a repair list ranked by yearly loss, which copies into a maintenance ticket. The audio never leaves the phone.

### Expected environmental benefit

A measured tap gets fixed sooner, and the ranked list puts the worst leak first. In the app, the sample residence of ten taps loses about 64,000 litres a year across its nine open taps, plus the energy to heat the hot ones.

### Tools used

Next.js 15, React 19, TypeScript 5, Tailwind CSS 4, GSAP 3 with `@gsap/react` 2, zod 4, the Web Audio API and Vercel. Constants come from USGS (drop size), UK DESNZ (natural gas factor) and Ember (grid carbon intensity). AI coding assistants were used for scaffolding and code; the detector design, product decisions and final review are ours, and the product runs no AI model.

### Limitations

Echo, extractor fans and running water elsewhere can cause false counts, and two taps in one room look like one noisy source. Without the spoon the result rests on the 0.25 mL default. Hot water energy uses a temperature rise you set and a fixed heater efficiency, grid factors are yearly national averages, and we do not count the energy to pump and treat the water yet. The detector bench uses synthetic recordings; the starting repair list is sample data for Ayazağa Student Residence.

### What we learned

Phone microphones ship with filters built for voices, and echo cancellation and noise suppression treat a drip as noise to remove, so they have to be turned off. Drop size matters as much as the rate: for the story tap in the app, the teaspoon moves the yearly figure from about 6,800 to about 8,500 litres.

### What's next

Move capture to an AudioWorklet and test on more phones, record and hand-count real taps for a field accuracy table, and share one repair list across a residence.
